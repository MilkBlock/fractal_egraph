\begin{abstract}
Equality saturation shares concrete expressions, but repeated derivations with
different bindings can still generate large graphs and dense execution histories.
We present a hierarchy that treats rule applications as evidence for reusable
reasoning units. Committed events form a versioned dependency graph. Relative to
a selected context, applications are classified as coarse or smooth and organized
into coarse layers, smooth layers, and CS units. Sequential CSCS composition
preserves dependencies; guarded CCSS regrouping combines the coarse and smooth
parts without discarding interface obligations. An explicit \emph{ripen} operation
computes local closure under staged external inputs, while exact state comparison
preserves types, equality, visibility, and the relevant rule environment.
We then define parameterized fractal rule compositions through stable binding and effect
transfers, distinguishing well-founded dissipative processes from families with
arbitrarily long finite prefixes. These abstractions suggest finite scheduling,
endpoint reduction, and demand-driven symbolic feedback to ripen. A Rust/native prototype implements bounded candidate discovery, staged replay,
exact comparison of exported local states, resource subscriptions, and promotion of
validated compositions. In one fixed experiment it produced nine closed local
results, seven distinct representative bodies, and six promoted composition records at depth
two. These are isolated symbolic-interface results: no live tier0 replacement,
whole-program compression, or acceleration is implemented. General induction and
fractal-summary feedback remain proposals.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} equality saturation; rule composition; local closure;
relative binding; event-driven analysis; recursive summaries.

\section{Introduction}
An e-graph shares e-nodes with identical operators and canonical children, and
records equivalence between expressions through union. This eliminates concrete
structural duplication. It does not automatically share a derivation such as
expansion, commutation, reassociation, and folding across many different bindings.
Even when the expression graph shares some nodes, the history of matches and the
space of potential rule combinations may continue to grow.

Our objective is to discover reusable reasoning units that can eventually reduce
repeated storage and matching. Three objects must remain distinct: a state, a
process reaching that state, and the conditions enabling the process. Different
processes may reach the same state, while the same rule sequence may behave
differently under different bindings and external effects. Neither a list of rule
names nor a single endpoint expression is therefore an adequate sharing key.

Egglog combines Datalog-style reasoning with equality saturation and provides the
native execution substrate for our prototype~\cite{egglog}. We use \emph{tier0}
for ordinary execution, \emph{tier1} for the analysis of applications and their
compositions, and \emph{tier2} for binding transfers, recurrence evidence, and
symbolic reduction. These are logical layers: the current tier1 is primarily a
Rust data structure rather than a mandatory chain-shaped egglog encoding.

This report gives a bounded implementation account of the hierarchy and audits its
evidence boundaries. The implemented contribution is narrower than the surrounding
vocabulary: observed applications are grouped into candidate units, candidate
compositions are replayed in isolated native cells, and exported local states can be
compared and catalogued. The prototype does not replace tier0 execution, discover all
useful combinations, prove arbitrary-depth recurrences, or perform runtime storage
compression. The diagrams therefore distinguish observed history, replayed local
state, and proposed summaries instead of presenting them as one execution pipeline.

\subsection{How the mechanisms fit together}
Read the finite pipeline in this order: recorded applications supply evidence;
CSCS and CCSS propose candidate interfaces; native replay validates those interfaces;
ripen computes a local fixed point; exact comparison groups equivalent bodies.
These operations work on different objects. Promotion feeds a validated member
descriptor back into candidate discovery; it does not use the exported saturated
body as an executable macro. Sections 7--9 then develop parameterized families and
summary contracts as an extension of this finite pipeline. Their definitions and
examples remain useful even where the execution feedback is not yet integrated.

\section{State and Execution Evidence}
\subsection{Facts, equality, visibility, and updates}
We distinguish the components of a runtime state as
\begin{equation}
 G=(F,\equiv,V,\Theta,\delta).
\end{equation}
Here $F$ contains constructor and relation facts, $\equiv$ is equality,
$V$ records row visibility, $\Theta$ contains function tables and their update
semantics, and $\delta$ denotes execution and incremental-maintenance state.
The supplied rewrite theory is trusted; native replay is not an independent
proof of every rule as a mathematical identity. A union is not an ordinary row insertion; subsumption is not simply the absence
of a semantic fact; and a function update is not necessarily set union.

For a positive fragment that only adds facts and grows equality, a monotone
closure model is useful. General programs require a stronger operational
contract. A subsumed row can remain stored while ceasing to participate in
matching. Comparing only its contents would lose this distinction.

\MathEgraph

\subsection{Committed applications rather than matches}
An application record is modeled as
\begin{equation}
 a_i=(R_i,\beta_i,Q_i,\Delta_i,U_i,W_i),
\end{equation}
where $R_i$ is a rule, $\beta_i$ its binding, $Q_i$ the facts and equality evidence
read, $\Delta_i$ the committed writes, $U_i$ the union information, and $W_i$
update or visibility information. A logical match, a surviving action lane, an
inserted row, and a deduplicated write are different events. Our provenance must
not silently identify them.

An edge from $a_i$ to $a_j$ records that $a_j$ used a then-valid fact version or
equality witness supplied by $a_i$. Equal values can suggest interaction, but
value equality alone does not establish this causal edge. The event graph is
acyclic in time even when a rule consumes its own earlier output. Cycles in a
quotient graph of recurring patterns instead denote recursion between pattern
instances, not cycles in the original event history.

\subsection{Relative binding as an interface map}
A relative binding maps an input of one unit to an output, a carried input, or a
new external port:
\begin{equation}
 \rho:\operatorname{In}(B)\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Out}(A)\cup\operatorname{In}(A)\cup E.
\end{equation}
It also carries types, alias constraints, and equality evidence. Such a map may
be a permutation, a projection through constructor arguments, a construction of
new terms, or a reference to another application's result. The notation
$\beta'=\beta+\Delta\beta$ is appropriate only for an actually additive coordinate
system; it is not a general definition of relative binding.

Definitions and uses follow a dependency order. A later application may refer to
an earlier binding, but a forward reference or a collision between names from
different instances is invalid. Numbering variables by first appearance alone
is not a complete canonicalization of arbitrary branching graphs.

\MathBinding

\section{Coarse and Smooth Rule Compositions}
Let $H$ be a selected local unit. Immediately before its $i$th stage, let $A_i(H)$
be the evidence supplied by already injected inputs and earlier internal actions.
For the chosen recorded witness, define the new external obligation conceptually
as
\begin{equation}
 E_i(H)=Q_i\setminus A_i(H).
\end{equation}
The new injection for stage $i$ is not yet included in $A_i(H)$. By convention,
the initial triggering input is charged to the first coarse step.
This is evidence subtraction, not subtraction of bare IDs: equality, versions,
and visibility must be respected.

\begin{definition}[Context-relative application kind]
A one-step composition is a \emph{coarse rule composition} relative to $H$ if it requires a nonempty
new external obligation $E_i(H)$. It is a \emph{smooth rule composition} if all its premises
are explained by the current local interface and prior internal results.
\end{definition}

The classification belongs to an application in a chosen combination, not to the
rule permanently. A coarse application can become smooth when its suppliers are
included in a larger unit. Classification also depends on the selected witness:
a different internal proof may exist without having been discovered. Missing
provenance is not proof that no such internal derivation exists.

For the running example, take Add associativity and commutation from
\texttt{math.egg}. Starting from external $a+(b+c)$, associativity is coarse.
Commutation of its produced row $(a+b)+c$, with $x=[a+b]$ and $y=c$, is smooth
relative to that two-step unit. Viewed alone, the same commutation requires an
external row and is coarse. Figure~\ref{fig:math-guard} gives a second concrete
case: merely having the denominator binding does not provide its nonzero guard.

A nonempty-premise combination normally begins with a coarse injection. A rule
with no premises is a logical exception. An engineering convention such as
\code{Empty -> coarse rule composition} can provide a uniform entry representation, but does
not prove that every rule needs external data.

\section{Layers and CS Units}
A \emph{coarse layer} groups coarse applications with explicit binding anchors.
A \emph{smooth layer} contains applications whose relevant dependencies can be
explained behind that interface. We write a CS unit as
\begin{equation}
 U=(C,S,I,O,\Pi),
\end{equation}
with coarse members $C$, smooth members $S$, external interface $I$, output ports
$O$, and witness information $\Pi$.

Figure~\ref{fig:math-layers} makes the layer distinction concrete. The native
trace contains an associativity write that is subsequently read by commutation
with $x=[a+b]$ and $y=c$. A layer encloses applications; its outline must not be
confused with the blue/orange roles of facts inside each application.
\MathLayers

An intersection is a reason to inspect a pair, not automatically a reason to
merge it. For example, merging transitively on the shared constant zero can
destroy locality. A common smooth consumer, explicit port connections, candidate
budgets, and coverage checks provide stronger selection signals. Requiring a
consumer to use outputs from each participating coarse member is a useful
heuristic, not a semantic-equivalence theorem.

Units need not form a disjoint partition. A fact can be exposed by several
interfaces, and a history can admit multiple useful decompositions. Sharing
references and retaining alternatives avoids permanently committing to an early
partition.

A \emph{Use} is a concrete occurrence of an installed rule-combination template.
It records a template ID, member events, and actual port bindings. This differs
from a CS unit, which is defined by contextual obligations. The earlier pipeline
admitted only Uses to ripen. The current pipeline can create CS candidates even
when no template has been installed; Use remains an alternative candidate source
and an experimental baseline.

\section{Two Candidate Composition Operators}
\subsection{Sequential CSCS composition}
If $U_2$ consumes outputs of $U_1$, we represent their composition as
\begin{equation}
 \Seq(U_1,U_2,\rho).
\end{equation}
The map $\rho$ separates inputs supplied by $U_1$ from inputs that still require
external injection. It preserves the dependency direction rather than simply
concatenating two event arrays. If both units contain applications depending on
intermediate results of the other, the complete units cannot be placed in a
simple sequential order. A finer decomposition or an unknown result is needed.
Dependencies through events outside the proposed pair must also be checked.

\MathCSCS
The multiplication context in Figure~\ref{fig:math-cscs} is a genuinely new
external obligation. The carried Add$(a,b)$ fact is also explicit: omitting it
would incorrectly classify the later inner distribution as self-contained.
The diagram shows an interface-level grouping of a native trace; a runtime
heuristic may choose a finer layer cut.

\subsection{Guarded CCSS regrouping}
The second operator regroups the coarse and smooth parts:
\begin{equation}
 \Par(U_1,U_2,\alpha)
   = (C_1\cup C_2,\ S_1\cup S_2,\ I',O',\Pi').
\end{equation}
Here $\alpha$ gives typed anchors between the C parts. The symbol $\Par$ names
regrouping, not a parallel execution schedule. Dependencies within the combined C or S DAG remain edges.

Our sufficient admission conditions are conservative: disjoint member events,
an explicit coarse-port intersection, supported positive
constructor/relation/equality operations, and no transitive dependency from
any S member to any C member. An exhausted bounded search is unknown, not evidence
of independence.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional regrouping]
Fix the external-input obligations and a complete dependency witness. If no C
member depends on an S member, a topological order exists that places C before S.
For supported positive monotone actions, changing between legal internal orders
preserves the resulting deductive closure.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All predecessors of C lie outside S, so the C subgraph can be topologically
ordered before the S subgraph. Every selected premise remains available because
internal producers precede consumers and the external obligations are fixed.
Independent additions and unions do not retract earlier evidence. Recomputing
closure under the same positive theory therefore preserves the consequences.
\end{proof}

This proposition does not move a late external fact earlier. The implementation
validates the original staged sequence; its CCSS certificate is conditional on
interface obligations, not a claim that the surrounding tier0 execution has been
rescheduled. Function updates, subsume, and unsupported guards do not pass the
positive-fragment regrouping check.

\MathCCSS

\subsection{Anchors need not be an initial intersection}
Suppose one unit supplies $P(a,b)$, another supplies $Q(c,d)$, and a recorded
consumer also reads $Link(b,c)$. The consumer and the Link fact anchor a
combination even if the original C parts shared no node. This is a more general
interaction witness than a direct coarse intersection. It must not be forced
into the particular CCSS admission test.

The prototype stores explicit composition references. After successful saturation,
a descriptor retaining the source members can be promoted and become an operand
of later candidate compositions. Later jobs flatten member references and replay
them; they do not merge the previously exported bodies as execution inputs. Their inherited coarse
and smooth descriptions are still conservative: promotion does not automatically
find the best reclassification or all legal regroupings.

\section{Ripen and Saturation}
\subsection{Local closure under staged inputs}
Given a rule environment $\mathcal R$, a local entry $I$, and an injection plan
$J$, ripen replays the observed preconditions, injections, and actions, then runs
the local rules until a complete sweep makes no update or a budget is reached.
The statuses are deliberately distinct:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{Pending}: not yet processed;
\item \code{Suspended}: insufficient execution budget;
\item \code{Rejected}: entry or validation obligations were not met;
\item \emph{Saturated}: an operational fixed point under the specified local model.
\end{itemize}

A local saturation result neither saturates all of tier0 nor certifies every embedding
of the interface. \code{RipenInput(k)} is an opaque symbolic boundary node: a
repeated index preserves identity, while different indices are initially
unmerged. It is not a wildcard that matches every value. It does not recover the
internal structure of an external eclass. Matches requiring that structure must
be treated as additional coarse interaction.

If a second C requires a later external fact, the contract has the form
\begin{equation}
 I_1;C_1;S_1;\operatorname{Inject}(I_2);C_2;S_2.
\end{equation}
Injection declares a caller obligation; it does not derive the fact internally.
Promoting $I_2$ into the initial entry without justification would change the
activation conditions.

\begin{definition}[Saturated rule composition]
A saturated rule composition consists of a validated composition, its trigger
and input mapping, its staged obligations, a saturated realization $K$, and its
certificate. In the current implementation $K$ is a finite materialized body.
Cataloguing equal $K$ states does not identify two compositions with different triggers or
obligations, and no live matcher reuse is performed. A symbolically saturated infinite body needs the stronger query
contract discussed later.
\end{definition}
\MathSaturated

\subsection{Sharing contents without conflating triggers}
Different combinations may export identical local states while retaining different
triggers, bindings, and injection plans; the current catalog records this equality but
does not install shared bodies into tier0. The prototype compares
complete exported typed graphs, equality classes, fixed ports, and row visibility.
The rule environment must match. When updates or visibility matter, registration
order and ruleset scheduling information are also retained.

\begin{proposition}[Composition of monotone closures]
If $\Cl$ is extensive, monotone, and idempotent over a fixed universe, then
\begin{equation}
 \Cl(\Cl(A)\cup\Cl(B))=\Cl(A\cup B).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Extensiveness gives $A\cup B\subseteq\Cl(A)\cup\Cl(B)$, yielding one inclusion
by monotonicity. Conversely, both $\Cl(A)$ and $\Cl(B)$ are contained in
$\Cl(A\cup B)$. Monotonicity and idempotence give the reverse inclusion.
\end{proof}

This identity motivates a possible future algorithm starting from existing closures
and processing new interaction. The current pipeline instead reconstructs and
replays members; it does not implement this identity as a body-composition algorithm. Their union need not already be saturated. The identity supplies no
execution budget and is not a theorem about every egglog operation. In
particular, subsume changes visibility, and a min/max merge alone does not prove
that every surrounding query is monotone.

Exported-content equality is also not equivalence of arbitrary suspended
incremental engines: the key does not serialize all of $\delta$. Direct reuse of
native matcher or join state is not implemented. The current guarantee is sharing
of declared local contents with separate trigger contracts.

\subsection{Versioned evidence and recursive promotion}
Historical saturated bodies are immutable; the applicability of a concrete
instance is separately versioned. Token, table, and scope subscriptions are
notified by committed changes. Union joins subscriptions so later notifications
follow aliases. Updates and subsumption expire affected evidence. Unknown row
ownership causes a conservative table- or scope-level invalidation.

A stale descriptor can propose another symbolic candidate, but cannot bypass
native staged validation. Certificates retain \code{live\_substitution=false}:
a lease whose version has not changed is not a proof of a current whole-graph
embedding. Successful saturated compositions are promoted as new descriptors and
registered for further pairing, without replacing tier0 facts.

\section{Parameterized Rule Compositions}
\subsection{Stable binding and effect transfers}
Repeated rule names are weak evidence. Two ABAB sequences can have different
bindings; conversely, independently scheduled branches can represent the same
dependency structure despite different event orders. We model a repeated step as
\begin{equation}
 z_{k+1}=T(z_k),\qquad z_k=(\beta_k,\mathcal E_k,\mathcal P_k),
\end{equation}
where $\beta_k$ is the binding, $\mathcal E_k$ accumulated effects, and
$\mathcal P_k$ the frontier of positions and obligations. In an operational fragment, the effect component must retain
structured updates and visibility, not merely a set of inserted facts.

A fractal rule composition is a parameterized family with a trigger, guard, stable transfer,
effect contract, and observable interface. ``Fractal'' denotes recursive
structural regularity here, not a claimed geometric dimension. A useful abstract
form is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F=(\mathit{Trigger},D,T,\mathit{Guard},\mathit{Effects},\mathit{Queries}).
\end{equation}
Finite histories propose such an object; they do not prove that it remains valid
for arbitrary counts. The current transfer analysis records Carry, Return, and
External port origins and finite witnesses. General induction remains a separate
proof obligation.

\MathFractalBinding

\subsection{Trigger states and nonuniform prefixes}
A stable phase may begin only after several injections and effect-producing
steps:
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Trigger}(I,\beta_0);T^k;\operatorname{Exit}.
\end{equation}
Different bindings may require different triggers. An initial ``dirty'' prefix
must not be omitted merely because the later transfer is regular.

For example, swapping Add arguments twice is an identity on argument positions,
but the first swap may insert a node and perform a union. For a fixed position
and fixed arguments, once both orders and their equivalence are present, further
swaps add no local effect. The stabilization point depends on the initial graph;
there is no universal rule equating two swaps with an empty execution.

Nor does a change of rule name in the log establish a turning point. Semi-naive
scheduling and other work can interrupt a still-enabled family. A turning point
must be defined by a guard, resource, interface, or effect change. Fractal
operators need not commute or be orthogonal.

\MathTrigger

\subsection{Multiple application positions}
For a branching family, use an indexed position domain rather than flattening all
steps into a sequence. A schematic binary generator can use
\begin{equation}
 D_{k+1}=\{2i,2i+1\mid i\in D_k\}.
\end{equation}
The text $A\to B+B$ alone does not prove physical doubling: identical B nodes
can be hashconsed. Exponential e-graph growth additionally requires distinct
bindings or positions that are not absorbed by existing equality. Three-way
branching generally suggests $3^k$, not cubic growth in $k$.

We must therefore distinguish unfolded tree size, parameterized description
size, event count, and deduplicated e-node count. A compact binding generator can
represent a large family even when the resulting graph does not compress by the
same factor.

\section{Dissipative and Infi Rule Compositions}
\subsection{Dissipation is a property of obligations}
\begin{definition}[Dissipative Rule Composition]
Under a fixed external-supply contract, a fractal process is dissipative if a
well-founded measure strictly decreases on every valid recursive step. A simple
case is $\mu(z_k)\in\mathbb N$ with
$\mu(z_{k+1})<\mu(z_k)$.
\end{definition}

The measure applies to progress steps of the represented process; redundant
re-execution of an already accounted match is not a new recursive step.
The decreasing quantity may count remaining positions, an unprocessed index
range, or outstanding obligations. It does not necessarily count facts being
deleted from the e-graph. Facts are generally reusable, so a need for external
facts does not itself imply dissipation. Replenishing the external supply may
invalidate a phase-local ranking argument.

For a finite sum,
\begin{equation}
 (i,s)\mapsto(i+1,s+x_i),\qquad 0\le i<n,
\end{equation}
$\mu=n-i$ proves termination. In reducing five ones to five, it is the remaining
reduction positions that decrease. The constant node one can remain a single
shared node throughout, and need not disappear at all.

A branching process can also be dissipative while its levels become wider.
Suppose each task of rank $r>0$ creates at most $d$ tasks of rank at most $r-1$,
and rank-zero tasks terminate. On a finite frontier, use
\begin{equation}
 \mu=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}(d+1)^{r(p)}.
\end{equation}
Replacing a positive-rank task adds at most $d(d+1)^{r-1}$, less than the removed
weight $(d+1)^r$. Thus bounded-depth binary expansion can have exponentially wide
levels and still terminate. The removed objects in this argument are pending
tasks, not retained e-graph facts.

Repeated integration by parts offers another candidate: polynomial degree may
decrease if the correct factor and variable are repeatedly differentiated.
This needs a polynomial and differentiation contract; finite examples do not
prove that arbitrary integral rewrites terminate. We do not report such a general
proof as an implemented capability.

\MathDissipative

\subsection{Infi rule composition as an unbounded prefix interface}
\begin{definition}[Infinite-prefix family]
For a fixed admitted trigger instance, an infi rule composition admits, under its
invariant, a valid prefix of length $k$ for every natural number $k$. Claiming unbounded growth of the physical
graph further requires a freshness argument for facts or positions.
\end{definition}

The initial instance is fixed: choosing a larger terminating input separately
for each $k$ does not establish this property. This definition concerns a family
of finite prefixes. With nondeterministic
choices, arbitrarily long paths do not by themselves establish that one selected
execution continues forever. A fixed transfer and consistent prefixes provide a
stronger contract.

We distinguish stabilization of effects, bounded periodic representations, and
genuinely unbounded generation. Pure smooth execution does not imply the last
case, and budget exhaustion proves none of them. Unknown remains an essential
outcome.

For example, mathematical-natural-number payloads can describe
$A(k,i)\to B(k,2i),B(k,2i+1)$ and $B(k,i)\to A(k+1,i)$.
Machine i64 arithmetic additionally requires overflow guards or bounded depth;
it cannot inherit an unbounded mathematical conclusion without qualification.

\subsection{A productive example from math.egg}
Let $D_x u$ denote \code{Diff(x,u)}, let $A_x v$ denote
\code{Integral(v,x)}, and define $I_k=\operatorname{Integral}(u_kv_k,x)$.
The actual integration-by-parts rewrite is:
\begin{lstlisting}
(rewrite (Integral (Mul a b) x)
  (Sub (Mul a (Integral b x))
       (Integral (Mul (Diff x a) (Integral b x)) x)))
\end{lstlisting}
It gives the uniform transfer
\begin{equation}
 u_{k+1}=D_xu_k,\qquad v_{k+1}=A_xv_k,\qquad
 I_k\Rightarrow u_kv_{k+1}-I_{k+1}.
\end{equation}
In the IBP-only fragment, with distinct symbolic Var leaves and no additional
equalities, the derivative and antiderivative arguments grow structurally.
The residual is again an eligible Integral-of-Mul node. The two constructors
are never simplified by another rule, so this supplies arbitrarily long finite
prefixes with fresh nodes. No numeric loop counter is evaluated in the graph.
\MathInfi
In this fragment, each step creates one Diff row, one antiderivative Integral,
one residual Integral, two Mul rows, and one Sub row. Distinct derivative and
antiderivative arguments prevent their keys from coalescing; IBP unions the
integral result with its Sub representative, not with either argument. Thus the
constructor-row count is $N_{\rm Math}(k)=5+6k$, excluding primitive String values
and implementation-only global bindings.
A native check of prefixes $k=0,1,2,3,4$ gives $5,11,17,23,29$ Math e-nodes.
These finite measurements check the illustration; the all-depth argument depends
on the restricted theory. In full \texttt{math.egg}, derivative simplification,
other integration rules, and pruning can stop or change the recurrence. With a
polynomial derivative contract, the same IBP pattern can instead become dissipative.


\subsection{A recursive DSL that becomes an array contract}
The previous examples use rules copied from \texttt{math.egg}. The following
four small programs are deliberately written as a new recursive DSL. They use
only a cursor, an upper bound, and a ternary expansion; no array constructor is
present in the source. Bake observes the native traces, extracts the repeated
binding/effect transfer, and then applies its two structural array-law checks.
The generated library is therefore a useful test of the abstraction boundary:
the array appears after recognition, not as a hand-written replacement rule.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={The two source families supplied to Bake}]
; scan-short.egg and scan-long.egg
(datatype Math (Cursor i64 i64) ...)
(rule ((= node (Cursor i end)) (< i end))
      ((Cursor (+ i 1) end)) :name "scan-next")

; tree-one.egg and tree-four.egg
(datatype Math (Expand i64) (Emit i64))
(rule ((= node (Expand n)) (> n 0))
      ((Emit (+ (* n 3) 0))
       (Emit (+ (* n 3) 1))
       (Emit (+ (* n 3) 2))) :name "split-three")
(rule ((= node (Emit n))) ((Expand n)) :name "resume")
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Feedback from Fractal Compositions to Ripen}
\subsection{Finite scheduling and reduction}
A proved dissipative family supplies a principled local count or budget. Ripen
can execute its trigger, finite repeat, and exit as a unit, rechecking the
contract when external supply or binding changes. A ranking for one family does
not prove that all of ripen terminates: another family may reactivate it.

The existing tier2 DSL includes an explicit Reduce node and exact endpoint laws.
For a natural count $k$ and a fixed per-step contribution $c$,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Reduce}(\operatorname{AddConstant}(c),k,t)=t+kc.
\end{equation}
For contributions $a+id$, $i=0,\ldots,k-1$, the accumulated contribution is
\begin{equation}
 ka+\frac{k(k-1)}{2}d.
\end{equation}
These are laws for a supplied accumulation contract, not an algorithm discovering
that contract from arbitrary history. They concern exact algebra; floating-point
reassociation and machine overflow require additional justification.

\subsection{A worked endpoint and intermediate-fact example}
Consider the guarded recurrence
\begin{equation}
 R:\ f(t)\Rightarrow f(t-1)+1,\qquad t>0.
\end{equation}
For natural $n$ and $0\le k\le n$, induction yields the endpoint
$f(n-k)+k$. Setting $k=n-m$ gives
\begin{equation}
 f(n)\Rightarrow f(m)+n-m,\qquad 0\le m\le n.
\end{equation}
The guard provides a dissipative measure. Removing it over mathematical integers
instead permits an unbounded descending sequence, subject to a freshness and
effect argument; the same machine-integer implementation is still bounded by its
numeric semantics.

The endpoint does not contain every intermediate fact. Another rule may need
$f(n-j)$ at a particular $j$. A reusable summary therefore needs a query interface
for $0\le j\le k$, together with the corresponding equality/effect information.
Replacing the entire history with only the last expression can suppress such
interactions and is not a sound implementation of general ripen.

\MathSummary
For the IBP example, the actual unrolling is nested Sub nodes. Turning that
nesting into the alternating-sum endpoint requires additional exact additive
laws. The IBP-only native trace is not claimed to perform this normalization.

\subsection{Arrays and symbolic coverage}
A useful summary contract exposes a trigger, iteration domain, binding transfer,
effects, endpoint queries, and intermediate-position queries. Finite domains
naturally give fold and array abstractions; infinite-prefix families require
lazy instantiation. Existing logical ArrayGet, Tabulate, Map, and Fold constructs
provide relevant vocabulary, but do not by themselves implement mutable memory,
alias analysis, cache placement, or GPU scheduling.

An infi rule composition that continually generates fresh facts generally has no finite
materialized saturated body. We propose a distinct \code{SymbolicallyCovered} status:
within a specified observation interface, internal matches and effects are
represented by a summary and required positions can be produced on demand.
This needs both sound answers and coverage of relevant internal matches. If a
query cannot be answered, ripen must retain a frontier, expand the requested
position, or return unknown. It must not label budget-limited sampling as closure.

\subsection{Bake and the present integration boundary}
Bake can aggregate observed families from multiple examples into a frozen
library. A new example matches the library under explicit binding/effect
contracts rather than silently assuming that training examples proved generality.
The local implementation already includes narrow, name-independent proof schemas
for a guarded unit-stride scalar recurrence and a bounded radix-frontier payload
query~\cite{code}. The former preserves the limit and other fields; the latter
checks residue coverage and machine-range bounds. Their certified queries do not
materialize all tier0 effects.

These narrow queries are useful evidence that executable summaries are possible.
They are not a complete fractal rule composition-to-ripen integration. The current native
ripen path still reports zero fractal summaries used. General induction and a
complete internal-match interface remain open.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Target feedback protocol. The certified-summary branches are not yet generally integrated.}]
on committed event e:
    notify subscribers of changed resources
    update witnessed CS interfaces
    propose bounded CSCS and guarded CCSS candidates

for candidate U within budget:
    validate staged external obligations and bindings
    if a verified dissipative summary applies:
        guide ripen using its finite domain and effect contract
    else if a verified infinite summary covers required queries:
        retain SymbolicallyCovered and a demand-driven frontier
    else:
        run ordinary bounded ripen
    catalogue equal exported states, retaining trigger obligations
    promote certified results and notify subscribers
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Implementation audit: what the code actually establishes}
The implementation boundary is narrower than the terminology used by the diagrams.
\path{native_saturated_rule_composition_pipeline} creates bounded jobs for Uses, dependency candidates, CSCS,
and CCSS, then calls \path{native_ripen} on an isolated entry program. Every job is
replayed and validated again; stale evidence cannot authorize substitution. A
successful job exports a \texttt{SaturatedRuleComposition} and may promote a composition descriptor in
the in-memory candidate store. \path{saturated_rule_composition::compare} checks a bounded typed graph
isomorphism including fixed ports and the subsumed-row mask. It does not compare
matcher state, scheduler state, or the surrounding tier0 e-graph.

The storage experiment in \path{experiments/closed_storage} is a separate Python tuple
store. Its 20.2 percent retained-object reduction is not egglog RSS, is not a live
kernel replacement, and comes with slower queries and union rebuilds. The native
pipeline reports \path{fractal_summaries_used=0}; the Bake and tier2 schemas export or
check narrow symbolic summaries but are not general feedback into ripen. The words
\emph{shared body}, \emph{saturated composition}, and \emph{fractal rule} therefore refer to
conditional artifacts or proposals unless a passage names the exact native check
that supports them.

\subsection{Bookkeeping cost and its limits}
Let $q$ be changed resources, $s$ subscription hits, and $w$ awakened or newly
promoted units. After interfaces have been formed, let $L$ bound interface work,
$K$ candidate partners, and $B$ the work of a local dependency check. A conditional
bookkeeping estimate is
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(q\log N_{\mathrm{res}}+s\log N_{\mathrm{unit}}+w(L+KB)\right),
\end{equation}
where the two $N$ values count tracked resources and units. This excludes interface
construction, environment checks, serialization, state-isomorphism checks, and the sum of native ripen
costs. A dense subscription can still wake many units. Attempt budgets do not
bound the worst-case time of one native rule call; the elapsed budget is checked
between jobs. These limits prevent a bookkeeping estimate from becoming an
unsupported end-to-end complexity claim.

\subsection{Recursive promotion and locality}
The fixed recursive-CS experiment contains three source chains and is processed by
bounded isolated ripen jobs. It reports nine closed jobs, seven equal exported
bodies, and six promotion records at maximum composition depth two. Its counters
include six layer visits, 75 pair checks, 14 resource notifications, and three
subscriber wakes~\cite{evidence}. A promotion adds a new composition descriptor to
the bounded candidate store; it does not install a macro into tier0 or substitute a
shared body during native matching.

This establishes that validated composition records can be fed back into the
candidate store. It does not establish compression, acceleration, or reuse by the
live egglog matcher. In a separate
registry test, union of two resources wakes their two subscribers among 100
independent units. Shared constants, common tables, or unknown mutation ownership
can legitimately require more notification work.

For a single snapshot, if $N(U_j)$ is the e-node set covered by instance $j$,

\begin{equation}

\end{equation}
Multiplying instance counts by template sizes overcounts overlap. Net storage
must also include dictionaries, bindings, residual facts, indexes, certificates,
versions, and any retained raw history. Shared output identities must remain
consistent across instances.

\section{Limitations and Research Agenda}
Finite recurrence evidence is not induction. Frequency and compression ratio do
not replace guard preservation, a ranking argument, or query completeness.
Likewise, recognizing a dissipative family does not prove that other rules cannot
restart it.

Exported-state equality and live instance substitution remain separate. Opaque
parameters omit external structure; version tracking prevents unchecked reuse but
does not itself validate a current whole-graph embedding. Subsumption and mutable
tables require their operational contracts rather than the pure closure identity.

The discovery procedure is intentionally incomplete. Candidate and dependency
bounds control work, and its cost depends on changed resources, subscription
hits, and bounded pairing rather than on a promise to find every useful subgraph.
Source-environment checks and report construction still have costs. Deeper
recursive descriptions can also increase maintenance overhead.

The next evaluation should run all variants at one revision, with matched inputs
and budgets, separately ablating Use admission, CSCS, CCSS, recursive promotion,
subscriptions, and certified fractal summaries. It should measure repeated
internal matching, retained memory, interface-query cost, invalidation/revalidation
cost, and omitted candidates, including workloads on which the method loses.

This report uses locally available source and bibliography metadata only. It does
not provide an exhaustive related-work survey or establish novelty priority over
unreviewed work on graph grammars, library learning, or recursive summaries.

\section{Conclusion}
We describe a bounded analysis pipeline for locality in recorded rule history. Coarse
and smooth labels, CSCS/CCSS candidates, staged replay, and exact comparison of
exported local states are implemented under explicit budgets and contracts. The
result is a catalog of conditional symbolic-interface observations; it is not a
replacement execution engine and it does not make equal local states interchangeable
inside tier0.

The recurrence and summary material defines proof obligations for future work. The
current code records finite transfer evidence and supports two narrow checked query
schemas, while the native ripen report records zero fractal summaries used. Proving
arbitrary-depth induction, covering all relevant internal matches, and measuring a
net runtime or storage benefit remain open.
